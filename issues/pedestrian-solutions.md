# Pedestrians hit at crossings — what works elsewhere, without more police

## The Israeli problem in one paragraph

2025: 122 pedestrians killed and 619 seriously injured, about a quarter of all road deaths (Road Safety Authority, [trends report](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/trends_2024/he/research_trends_2024_v03.pdf); [auto.co.il](https://www.auto.co.il/articles/car-news/local-news/road-safety-2025/)). 57 of the dead were 65+, the highest in a decade, and 84% of those died on city streets ([Or Yarok via CBS data, walla](https://news.walla.co.il/item/3767562)). Older people and children made up 53% of pedestrian deaths in the first half of 2025. So the problem is urban crossings, and the victims are mostly slow walkers.

## The principle every successful city uses

Do not ask people to behave perfectly; build streets where a mistake is not fatal ("Safe System", WHO). Speed at impact decides life or death: hit at 20 mph (32 km/h) about 1% of pedestrians die, at 35 mph (56 km/h) 19%, at 50 mph (80 km/h) over 80% ([IIHS](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/vehicle-height-compounds-dangers-of-speed-for-pedestrians)). Every solution below works by lowering speed at the crossing, making the pedestrian visible, or shortening the time they are exposed. None needs a police officer.

## Solutions with measured results

| # | Solution | What it is | Measured result | Cost / speed to build |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Daylighting** | Physically block parking in the last ~6–12 m before a crosswalk (bollards, planters, bike racks), so drivers and pedestrians see each other | Hoboken, NJ (60,000 people): no traffic deaths from Jan 2017 to at least 2024 after daylighting 130+ intersections and building 61 curb extensions; 88% of its pedestrian/bike crashes were at intersection crosswalks ([AP via Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2024/03/02/hoboken-new-jersey-no-traffic-fatalities-7-years-pedestrians), [Results for America case study](https://s3.amazonaws.com/cdn.catalog.results4america.org/content/uploads/Updated-2025-RFA_EMC_Case_Study_12_Hoboken_NJ_R1v1.pdf)). New York committed to 1,000 intersections a year | Very cheap, days per site |
| 2 | **Raised crosswalk** | The crosswalk is a speed table at sidewalk level; cars must slow to cross it | −45% pedestrian crashes ([FHWA STEP](https://highways.dot.gov/sites/fhwa.dot.gov/files/2022-06/Revised_STEP_Poster_Jan2020_revised_508compliant.pdf)) | Cheap, weeks |
| 3 | **Pedestrian refuge island** | An island in the middle, so you cross one direction at a time | −32% pedestrian crashes; raised medians at marked crossings −46% ([FHWA STEP](https://highways.dot.gov/sites/fhwa.dot.gov/files/2022-06/Revised_STEP_Poster_Jan2020_revised_508compliant.pdf)) | Medium |
| 4 | **Leading pedestrian interval** | The walk light turns green 3–7 seconds before the cars' green, so pedestrians are already in the crossing when turning cars arrive | New York, 6,003 intersections: about a third fewer pedestrian injuries, and −65% fatal pedestrian crashes in daylight ([Columbia, Nature Cities 2025](https://www.publichealth.columbia.edu/news/new-york-city-intersections-see-one-third-fewer-pedestrian-injuries-longer-head-start-intervals)). An earlier federal study found a smaller effect ([FHWA](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/18060/18060.pdf)) | Almost free: a signal-timing change |
| 5 | **Road diet** | 4 lanes become 2 + a centre turn lane (or a refuge), shorter crossings, slower traffic | −19% to −47% total crashes ([FHWA STEP](https://highways.dot.gov/sites/fhwa.dot.gov/files/2022-06/Revised_STEP_Poster_Jan2020_revised_508compliant.pdf)) | Paint first, concrete later |
| 6 | **30 km/h city default** | 30 km/h on residential streets, 50 only where marked | Helsinki: zero pedestrian deaths in 2019, then 12 months with no road deaths at all (2024–25), after most streets went to 30 plus a long-term Vision Zero plan ([City of Helsinki](https://www.hel.fi/en/news/no-pedestrian-fatalities-in-helsinki-traffic-last-year), [EU observatory](https://urban-mobility-observatory.transport.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/helsinki-records-zero-road-deaths-over-past-year-2025-08-22_en)). Wales (20 mph default, 2023): −11.8% casualties in the first year ([Transport for Wales](https://tfw.wales/national-monitoring-report-july-2025)); results still debated | Signs and street design; works best with 1–5 |
| 7 | **Vehicle design** | Automatic emergency braking that detects pedestrians; lower, sloped front ends | Pedestrian AEB cuts pedestrian crashes by 27%; vehicles with hoods over ~1 m are about 45% more likely to kill a pedestrian ([IIHS](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/vehicles-with-higher-more-vertical-front-ends-pose-greater-risk-to-pedestrians)). The EU now requires AEB and speed assistance on new cars (to verify: Regulation 2019/2144) | National: import standards and vehicle tax |

## Speed bumps: put them at the crossing, not before it

A bump 50 m before a crosswalk slows cars at the bump; they speed up again by the crossing. A raised crosswalk is the bump *at* the crossing: −45% pedestrian crashes (row 2). Israel already builds many bumps, so the ask is about placement and standard, not new money.

## "One car stopped, the next one didn't" (the multiple-threat crash)

On a road with two lanes in the same direction, the first car stops; the stopped car hides the pedestrian from the driver in the next lane, who often never sees them. It is illegal not to stop, but many of these drivers simply cannot see. Fixes that remove the blind spot:

- **Advance stop line**: a "stop here" line 6–15 m before the crosswalk, so the stopped car does not hide the pedestrian. −25% pedestrian crashes ([FHWA STEP](https://highways.dot.gov/sites/fhwa.dot.gov/files/2022-06/Revised_STEP_Poster_Jan2020_revised_508compliant.pdf); [FHWA guide](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/15043/008.cfm)).
- **Flashing beacons at the crossing**, pressed by the pedestrian (rectangular rapid-flashing beacon): −47% pedestrian crashes; all nine reviewed studies found drivers yield more ([FHWA toolbox](https://highways.dot.gov/safety/pedestrian-bicyclist/safety-tools/toolbox-pedestrian-countermeasures-and-their-potential)).
- **One lane per direction at the crossing** (road diet, curb extension or refuge island): no second lane, no second car.
- **Raised crosswalk**: every lane has to slow down, not only the polite driver.

## Other ideas that fit Israel (less hard evidence, worth asking about)

- **Longer walk time for older people**: time crossings for a slower walking speed near clinics, synagogues, community centres and elder housing. Fits the 65+ data directly.
- **Lighting at the crosswalk itself** (lit from the driver's side, not just street lights), since many pedestrian deaths happen in the dark.
- **School streets**: close the street in front of a school to cars at drop-off and pick-up times (common in the UK and Paris).
- **Flashing beacons at unsignalised crossings** (rectangular rapid-flashing beacons, pedestrian hybrid beacons): in the US federal list of proven measures.

## Why this is a good fit for the campaign

- Most of it is **municipal**, cheap and fast: daylighting, raised crosswalks and signal timing do not need new laws or police.
- Israel already forbids parking near crosswalks; daylighting turns a rule nobody enforces into a **physical fact** that needs no enforcement.
- Each item gives an MK a concrete, checkable ask: "ask the Transport Ministry how many signalised crossings have a leading pedestrian interval, and set a target".

## Asks this research supports

1. Transport Ministry: publish how many signalised crossings have a leading pedestrian interval; make it the default where turning cars cross pedestrians.
2. Transport Ministry + municipalities: a daylighting standard (physical, not paint) for every crosswalk near schools, clinics and elder housing, with a yearly target, like New York's 1,000 a year.
3. Raised crosswalks as the default for new and rebuilt crossings on residential streets.
4. Pedestrian-crossing timing for older walkers at named locations.
5. Ministry: report how many new cars sold in Israel have pedestrian AEB; consider import/tax incentives.
