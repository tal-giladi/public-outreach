# Outreach plan — email is our only power, so we use all of it

Tal's approach, 19.11.2026: no budget, no organisation behind us. The one thing we can do at scale is send email. Every campaign should reach as many people as possible: not only the Knesset, but every official, organisation, journalist and citizen who can move the issue.

The plan below maximises reach **without** getting the project shut down. Two things can kill it overnight:

1. **The anti-spam law** (Communications Law, section 30A, see the next section). Each message sent in breach can cost up to **₪1,000** in a lawsuit, with no need to prove damage. 5,000 cold emails to private people = a theoretical ₪5 million exposure.
2. **Deliverability**: a new address sending thousands of unsolicited emails gets flagged as spam within days, and then even the MKs stop receiving us.

## What the law actually says (section 30A, read 19.11.2026)

Source: the statute text ([Wikisource, section 30א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7_%D7%94%D7%AA%D7%A7%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%AA_(%D7%91%D7%96%D7%A7_%D7%95%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%93%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9D)); verify against the official Knesset text). Not legal advice.

- "Advertising" (דבר פרסומת) has three forms: (1) a **commercial** message; (2) a message sent **to the general public** asking for a **donation or as propaganda (תעמולה)**; (3) a public message inviting people to call a phone number.
- We ask for no money and sell nothing, so (1) does not apply. **But (2) can**: the law defines תעמולה as "spreading ideas to influence attitudes or behaviour", which describes a campaign. It excludes a message with a **"political message"**, a term the law does not define. Whether a road-safety campaign counts as "political" is exactly the question for a lawyer.
- Consent needed for (2): prior, explicit, written (an email or a checkbox counts) — for email, SMS, fax and auto-dial.
- **Exception 1 — one-time invitation**: a single email to anyone, for propaganda, that only **asks them to agree** to receive our messages, is allowed.
- **Exception 2 — registered nonprofits (סעיף 30א(ב1))**: an **amuta (עמותה) or public-benefit company (חל"צ)** may send propaganda or donation emails **without prior consent**, as long as the recipient has not refused. Email only, not SMS.
- Every such message must start with the word **"תעמולה"** in the subject line, give the sender's name and contact details, and offer a free, simple way to refuse. A refusal must be honoured.
- Emails to officials at their official addresses about their public duties are individual letters, not messages "to the general public"; lobbying MKs is also the clearest case of a "political message". Low risk, but include the lawyer check.

**Writing to public servants**: no law limits a citizen's letters to officials about their public work. The Digital Communication with Public Bodies Law, 2018 (חוק תקשורת דיגיטלית עם גופים ציבוריים) even requires public bodies to accept inquiries by digital means ([text](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7_%D7%AA%D7%A7%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%AA_%D7%93%D7%99%D7%92%D7%99%D7%98%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%AA_%D7%A2%D7%9D_%D7%92%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%9D_%D7%A6%D7%99%D7%91%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%99%D7%9D)). The one limit is harassment: Communications Law section 30 makes it a crime to use a telecom channel to threaten, scare or harass. Our emails stay polite, factual, twice a week at most, and stop when asked.

**Decision (Tal, 19.11.2026): our campaigns are political messages** — they press elected officials and ministries to act on public policy — and so fall outside the "תעמולה" definition. Ring 3 may therefore be emailed directly. We still keep, in every email: Tal's real name and contact details, a one-click way to stop, honouring every "stop" at once, no bought lists, and a gradual sending rate (deliverability). Every address gets a recorded public source. A lawyer check is still worth an hour before the first large send to private people.

**Fallback if that reading is challenged**: registering an **amuta** is the single step that legally unlocks mass email to private people. Until then: Rings 1 and 2 as below, Ring 3 by consent and by one-time invitations.

So: officials and organisations get written to directly; private people are reached through consent and through each other. A lawyer should confirm the line before round 1 (see the end).

### Ring 3 — Private people (consent first, then they multiply us)

Under Tal's decision (political message), Ring 3 includes direct email to private people whose addresses are publicly listed for contact, always with an opt-out. Registering an amuta adds a second legal basis (30A(b1)). And we make it easy for people to come to us, and each one sends on our behalf, from their own address:

1. **Sign-up on the site**: "קבלו עדכון כשחבר הכנסת שלכם עונה". One field (email), explicit consent checkbox, confirmation email (double opt-in). This is our own list.
2. **"Write to your MK / mayor" in one click**: a page per campaign with a ready message (`mailto:` link, prefilled subject and text) addressed to the official. The citizen sends it from their own email. 1,000 citizens = 1,000 emails from real voters, which MK offices count far more than one email from us. No spam-law issue: each person writes in their own name.
3. **Forward to friends**: every update ends with "Forward this to 3 people who cross the street every day."
4. **Partners send to their own lists**: Or Yarok, parents' bodies and others already have consent from their audiences; we give them a ready text and the sign-up link.
5. **Media**: every published result ("X of 120 MKs answered") is a press story, and every story links to the sign-up.

## The campaign cycle (repeat for each campaign)

| Step | When | Ring | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Week −2 | — | Issue file, facts verified, emails written, method published |
| 2 | Week −1 | 2 | Personal emails to partner organisations and journalists: "this starts next week, here is the data" |
| 3 | Week 0 | 3 | Campaign page live: sign-up + one-click "write to your MK / mayor" |
| 4 | Weeks 1–6 | 1 | The email series to all officials (road safety: 12 emails, `campaigns/road-safety/`) |
| 5 | Weekly | 3 | Short update to the sign-up list: who answered this week, one action for them |
| 6 | Every 2 weeks | 2 | Data to journalists and partners: answer rates, new commitments |
| 7 | Week 8 | all | Results published; thank-you to officials who acted; next campaign announced |

## Sending capacity

| Tool | Limit | Cost | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gmail (new address) | ~500 emails/day | Free | Ring 1 at first: 120 MKs a day is fine |
| Google Workspace on our own domain | ~2,000/day | ~$6/month | Ring 1 at full size (~4,000 over 2–3 days) |
| An email service with free tier (e.g. Brevo, MailerLite) | Free up to a few hundred/day or ~1,000 subscribers | Free | Ring 3 sign-up list only (consented) |

Rules: our own domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up before the first big send; warm the address up (start with tens a day, grow over 2–3 weeks); one recipient per email for Rings 1–2; never buy or scrape lists of private people. Tal works in email marketing, so he knows these better than anyone; if the service used is his employer's, say so publicly to keep the independence statement true.
